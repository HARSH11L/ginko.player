HOW TO USE GINKO

From first launch
to your first playlist.

A simple guide for the parts of Ginko that matter most.

01

SET UP YOUR MUSIC

Open Ginko and choose the folders that contain your music. Ginko scans them locally and builds your Library.

02

PLAY & ORGANIZE

Use the Library, search, Favorites and Recently Played. Create playlists using songs from your selected folders.

03

KEEP PLAYING

Start a song and use the mini-player, lock-screen controls or Android notification controls while you use other apps.

04

OPEN PRIVATE SPACE

Private Space is intentionally discreet. In the player, tap the center artwork 7 times within a short window to reveal the private-space entry.

05

USE YOUR PIN

Ginko supports three PIN roles: PIN 1 opens Private Space A, PIN 2 opens the separate Private Space B, and PIN 3 is reserved for emergency wipe.

06

LOCK IT AGAIN

Leave Private Space when you're done. Auto-lock can also be configured in Settings so the private area doesn't remain open indefinitely.

SECURITY, WITHOUT THE HACKER AESTHETIC

Private files stay private.

Ginko uses Android Keystore-backed key protection and encrypted vault storage. The private areas are separate from the normal music library.

QUESTIONS

Straight answers.

What is Ginko Player?

A local-first Android music player with a separate encrypted private-space feature. The normal player and private spaces are designed as separate areas.

Does Ginko upload my music?

Ginko is designed around local music stored on your device. Your selected music folders are scanned locally rather than being a cloud music service.

How do I open Private Space?

From the normal player, tap the center artwork 7 times within a short window. This reveals the private-space entry without putting a large vault button on the main player.

What do the three PINs do?

PIN 1 opens Private Space A, PIN 2 opens Private Space B, and PIN 3 is the emergency-wipe PIN. Each PIN must be at least 4 digits and the three PINs must be different.

Can I use Ginko only as a music player?

Yes. The normal music player does not require you to use the private-space feature.

Does removing a music folder delete it?

No. Removing a folder from Ginko removes it from Ginko's library configuration; it does not delete the physical folder from your device.

Is the app ready?

Ginko Player is currently in development. The website describes the intended and implemented direction; release availability can change as development continues.

Where can I report bugs or suggest features?

Use the project's GitHub Issues page once the real repository link has been added to this website.
